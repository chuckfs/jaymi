Vision

Build the computer we’ve always wanted.

This document is **Target Architecture** — the long-term product vision.

Kernel progress (Current Implementation) is tracked in `ROADMAP.md` and `ARCHITECTURE.md`. Layers 0–6 of the architectural kernel are shipped; Layers 7–9 and most of the experience described below remain Target.

Computers have become incredibly powerful, yet they still expect people to think like machines.

We organize folders.

We remember filenames.

We search through applications.

We switch between tools.

We repeat ourselves to different AI assistants.

Our knowledge becomes fragmented across conversations, documents, emails, projects, and applications.

The computer knows where everything is.

The user knows what they’re looking for.

Jaymi exists to bridge that gap.

⸻

Our Belief

The future of computing is conversational.

People should not have to remember where information is stored.

They should simply describe what they are trying to accomplish.

Instead of asking:

“Which application should I open?”

The question becomes:

“What do I want to do?”

Jaymi figures out the rest.

⸻

Our Mission

Build an offline-first personal AI environment that becomes the primary interface between people and their computers.

Jaymi should understand:

* Projects
* Files
* Documents
* Messages
* Images
* Notes
* Source code
* Personal knowledge
* User intent

It should retrieve the right information, understand the current context, choose the appropriate capabilities, and help users complete their work naturally.

⸻

Our Philosophy

Jaymi is built on a simple idea:

Your computer should adapt to you—not the other way around.

Everything flows from this principle.

If a feature makes the computer feel more natural, it belongs.

If a feature adds unnecessary complexity, it does not.

⸻

Privacy

Artificial intelligence should not require giving up ownership of your digital life.

Jaymi is designed to work locally whenever possible.

Your files remain your files.

Your conversations remain your conversations.

Your knowledge belongs to you.

Cloud intelligence is optional—not required.

⸻

Knowledge Ownership

Knowledge created with AI should never become trapped inside individual applications.

Conversations.

Ideas.

Projects.

Research.

Documents.

Code.

These belong to the user.

Jaymi exists to preserve, organize, and connect that knowledge regardless of where it was originally created.

⸻

Intelligence Through Context

Jaymi does not attempt to remember everything at once.

Instead, it remembers where knowledge lives.

When asked a question, Jaymi gathers only the information that matters.

It searches.

It reads.

It understands.

Then it reasons.

Context is built intentionally—not through brute force.

⸻

One Interface

Whether the task is:

* Writing
* Coding
* Researching
* Searching
* Creating images
* Organizing files
* Reading documents
* Running terminal commands

The interaction should remain the same.

One conversation.

One interface.

Many capabilities.

⸻

Trust

Trust is earned through transparency.

Jaymi should explain where answers came from.

Important actions should be reviewed before they happen.

The user should always remain in control.

Automation should assist—not surprise.

⸻

Extensibility

Technology changes.

Models improve.

Tools evolve.

Jaymi is designed so that intelligence can be replaced without replacing the system itself.

Everything should be modular.

Everything should be replaceable.

Nothing should depend on a single company, model, or ecosystem.

⸻

The Long-Term Goal

The goal is not to build another chatbot.

The goal is to build a computer that understands its user.

A computer that remembers.

A computer that searches by meaning instead of filenames.

A computer that understands projects instead of folders.

A computer that feels less like software…

…and more like a partner.

⸻

Success

Jaymi succeeds when interacting with a computer no longer feels like navigating software.

Instead, it feels like having a conversation.

When users stop asking:

“Where did I save that?”

and begin asking:

“Can you find the thing I’m thinking of?”

the vision has become reality.
